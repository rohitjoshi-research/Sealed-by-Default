Who can see the faces a parked car records?

Parked cars with cameras film everyone who walks past.

Today the footage is either raw, so every face is visible to whoever holds the file, or blurred for good, which also destroys the evidence after real damage.

This study tests, in simulation, a third option: faces sealed by default and unsealed only with the owner or a court.

This is a simulation study. No real vehicle or real bystanders were recorded.

What this shows, and what it does not

Shows

In simulation, sealing gives the privacy of fail-closed blurring and the evidence of raw recording at the same time.

Per-person keys stop an unseal from exposing bystanders, more so in crowds.

The safety net holds as long as people are detectable at lower pixel density than faces are identifiable; three real dusk photos agree.

Does not show

No real vehicle, camera or bystander data. Identifiability is a geometric proxy, not a face-recognition result.

Detector, rain, fog and night factors are assumptions; latency and glare are not modelled.

No legal approval: zones, court keys and the register do not exist today.
